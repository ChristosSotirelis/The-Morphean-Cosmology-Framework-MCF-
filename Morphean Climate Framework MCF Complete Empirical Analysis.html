Canvas 20 — Plain-Language Explanation for Non-Scientists

🌍 In Simple Terms

Our climate is influenced by more than just carbon dioxide. Human activities have produced invisible particles in the air — tiny metals from technology, microplastics, fibers from cotton and plants, and leftover particles from historical atomic events.

The growth of power lines, towers, and connected devices produces electromagnetic fields. These fields interact with the tiny particles in the air, making them absorb energy. This causes subtle heating that adds up over time.

Particles eventually settle on oceans, land, and vegetation, changing temperatures and affecting how heat is stored. Nighttime temperatures rise faster than daytime because the heating continues even when the sun isn't shining.

Measurements from 1900 to 2025 show correlations between EM infrastructure, particle presence, and temperature increases. Laboratory tests confirm the physics. The patterns are backed by data and can be independently verified.
