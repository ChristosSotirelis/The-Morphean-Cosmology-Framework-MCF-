A 1-megaton fireball expands to ~1.7 km in diameter within 10 seconds and ascends vertically at 75–110 m/s, reaching ~7.2 km altitude within one minute. For multi-megaton detonations, the cloud penetrates the stratosphere, where it can circulate globally for months to years before depositing as fallout. Approximately 76% of total ²³⁹Pu fallout from high-yield tests was injected into the stratosphere.

The prompt thermal energy released per kiloton is:

$$Q_{prompt} = Y_{kt} \times 4.184 \times 10^{12} \; \text{Joules}$$
Where $Y_{kt}$ is yield in kilotons. For Castle Bravo (15,000 kt): $Q_{prompt} \approx 6.3 \times 10^{16}$ J

2.2 The Precipitation Filter: What Gets Washed Out vs What Gets Through

Not all particles that enter the stratosphere reach the poles. Precipitation — rain and snow — is the primary scavenging mechanism. 4. The Concentrator: Cryoconite — How Radioactive Particles Accumulate at the Ice Surface

Once fallout particles reach the polar regions and deposit onto glaciers, they do not simply wash away. They are concentrated and trapped by a biological-mineral matrix called cryoconite — a dark, biogenic sediment found on glacier surfaces worldwide.

What Cryoconite Is

Cryoconite granules are mixtures of mineral particles (80–98% by mass), organic matter, microorganisms, fungal mycelium, black carbon, and trapped atmospheric pollutants. They form dark patches or holes on glacier surfaces. Their dark color means they absorb solar radiation strongly, melting the underlying ice and forming water-filled cylindrical holes — cryoconite holes — which further concentrate the granules.

Microplastic Radiative Co-Forcing (Emerging, 2026): Atmospheric colored microplastic and nanoplastic particles, co-located with fallout radionuclides in polar ice archives since the 1960s, constitute a newly recognized climate forcing agent with global mean DRF = 0.039 W m⁻² (16.2% of black carbon, rising to 4.7× black carbon regionally). Their surface albedo-reduction effect on ice when deposited is an unquantified additional forcing not yet incorporated into IPCC models.

The hypothesis that atomic-era emissions directly drive polar melting through radioactive decay heating alone is not supported. The supported mechanisms are: albedo reduction by dark deposited particles, secondary source activation upon warming-driven melt, and the emerging MNP radiative forcing pathway. These together constitute a measurable anthropogenic perturbation that is distinct from and additive to greenhouse gas forcing.

11. Summary for Everyone — What This All Means

🧊 The Simple Version

Step 1 (Source): Nuclear bomb tests, especially the large ones in the 1950s and 1960s, threw billions of radioactive particles miles up into the sky — above the weather, into the stratosphere.

Step 2 (Transport): The wind — specifically the jet stream and a slow global circulation called the Brewer-Dobson — carried those particles toward the poles. This took anywhere from a few days to a year, depending on the season. The season mattered enormously: spring and autumn were "open gates" for particles to descend, while other seasons blocked transport. We now have ice core evidence of this at seasonal detail — the most precise confirmation ever published (Science Advances, October 2025).

Step 3 (Concentration): When the particles landed on glaciers, they got sucked into a dark biological sediment called cryoconite. Cryoconite looks like black mud on the ice. Because it's dark, it absorbs sunlight far better than white snow. This melts the ice beneath it. The more cryoconite, the more melting. At Novaya Zemlya — where the Soviet Union tested its largest bombs — the cryoconite has the highest radioactive contamination ever recorded on a glacier. And now, as that glacier melts, the old contaminated ice buried underneath is being exposed again, releasing the radioactivity it stored 60 years ago.

Step 4 (Co-factor): In the same layers of polar ice where fallout was found, scientists are also finding microplastics from the 1960s onward. A landmark study from 2026 showed that colored microplastics floating in the air absorb sunlight 74 times more efficiently than plain white plastic, warming the atmosphere at 16% the rate of the most powerful air pollutant (black carbon soot). On polar ice surfaces, the same physics applies.

Step 5 (The honest limits): The radioactive particles themselves do not heat the ice through their radioactivity — the concentrations are too small. The mechanism is the dark color of the particles reducing the ice's reflectivity. The dates match the ice core evidence. The question of exactly how much this contributed to observed polar melt — versus greenhouse gases, versus other factors — is what the final-stage research questions (Group B) are designed to answer using the cross-correlation pipeline.
